An electric blind that has stopped working is frustrating — but before calling a technician, there are several checks you can carry out yourself. Many apparent faults turn out to be simple issues that take just a few minutes to resolve.

This guide walks you through a systematic diagnostic process, from the simplest checks to the ones that do require professional attention.

## Step 1: Check the power supply

Before assuming the motor has failed, confirm it is receiving power.

- **Check the circuit breaker.** Go to your electrical panel and verify that the circuit feeding the blind has not tripped. Reset it if necessary and test the blind again.
- **Check any wall switches.** Some installations include a dedicated on/off switch for the blind circuit. Ensure it is in the on position.
- **Try a different socket or circuit** (if the motor has a plug). This confirms whether the problem is with the power supply or the motor itself.

If the motor has power but still does not respond, move to step 2.

## Step 2: Check the remote control

Remote control issues account for a surprisingly large proportion of "faulty blind" call-outs.

- **Replace the batteries.** Even if the remote's LED indicator lights up, weak batteries may not transmit a strong enough signal to the motor.
- **Check the distance and angle.** Radio remotes generally work at up to 20–30 metres in open space, but walls, mirrors, and metallic surfaces can significantly reduce range.
- **Try controlling the blind from the wall switch** (if one is fitted). If the blind responds to the wall switch but not the remote, the fault is in the remote, not the motor.
- **Re-pair the remote.** If the remote was accidentally reset or a new remote was paired without following the correct procedure, the motor may have lost its pairing. Consult your installation manual for the pairing procedure.

## Step 3: Check the limit switches

Limit switches define the upper and lower travel limits of the blind. If they are incorrectly set, the motor may appear unresponsive.

**Symptoms of a limit-switch problem:**
- The blind moves in one direction only
- The blind stops partway through its travel
- The motor hums but the blind does not move

Limit switches can usually be reset by pressing and holding specific button combinations on the remote. Consult the motor's manual for the exact procedure. This is a common post-installation issue, particularly after a power cut.

## Step 4: Listen to the motor

If the motor makes a sound when you press the remote but the blind does not move, the problem is likely mechanical rather than electrical.

**Possible causes:**
- The blind fabric has jammed in the guide rails (for cassette-type blinds)
- The winding tube has become disengaged from the motor
- A foreign object is obstructing the blind's travel
- The drive belt or connector between motor and tube has broken

In these cases, do not force the motor — continued operation under a mechanical obstruction will burn out the motor.

## Step 5: Check the smart home integration (if applicable)

If your blinds are controlled via a smart home platform (Google Home, Alexa, Apple HomeKit), a connectivity issue may be preventing commands from reaching the motor.

- **Check whether the hub is online.** Most hubs have a status LED — consult its manual if you are unsure what the indicator means.
- **Try controlling the blind directly from the remote.** If the remote works but the app does not, the issue is with the hub or connectivity, not the motor.
- **Restart the hub.** Unplug it for 30 seconds, then reconnect. This resolves many temporary connectivity faults.
- **Check your Wi-Fi or home network.** If other smart devices are also unresponsive, the issue may be with your router.

## Common faults and likely causes

| Symptom | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| No response to remote or switch | Power supply fault or motor failure |
| Responds to switch but not remote | Remote battery or pairing issue |
| Moves in one direction only | Limit switch needs resetting |
| Motor hums, blind does not move | Mechanical obstruction or disconnected drive |
| Works with remote, not with app | Hub or connectivity issue |
| Moves erratically or stops mid-travel | Limit switch fault or motor overheating |

## When to call a technician

Contact a professional if:

- The motor does not respond to any input after the checks above
- You can smell burning or notice discolouration around the motor housing
- The motor makes a grinding or scraping noise
- The wiring or connections appear damaged
- The blind has dropped suddenly (possible spring or counter-balance failure in exterior blinds)

**Do not attempt to open the motor housing yourself.** Electric blind motors operate at mains voltage, and unauthorised opening will void the warranty. Internal repairs must be carried out by a qualified technician.

## Prevention: reducing the risk of faults

- Do not obstruct the blind's travel path — furniture, curtains, and window handles are common culprits
- Clean guide rails periodically to prevent dust build-up causing friction
- Avoid running the motor beyond its travel limits repeatedly (this is what limit switches prevent)
- After a power cut, check and reset the limit switches if the blind behaves unexpectedly

## Conclusion

Most electric blind faults are either power-supply issues, remote-control problems, or limit-switch settings — all of which you can check and often resolve yourself. If the motor itself has failed, a professional assessment will quickly confirm whether it can be repaired or needs replacing. With proper maintenance and correct installation, a quality motor should give you ten or more years of trouble-free service.
