The motor is the most important component of any electric blind system. It is what you will hear (or not hear) every morning when you open the blinds, and it is what will determine whether the system is still working reliably in ten years' time. Choosing correctly from the outset saves both money and frustration.

## Types of motor: what is available?

### Tubular motors
The most common type for roller blinds, solar screens, and exterior blinds. The motor is housed inside the winding tube and is virtually invisible once installed.

Tubular motors come in three broad quality tiers:

| Tier | Characteristics | Typical lifespan |
|---|---|---|
| Entry-level | Noisy, limited spare parts, basic warranty | 3–5 years |
| Mid-range | Quiet, reliable, 2-year warranty | 8–12 years |
| High-end | Very quiet, bidirectional, advanced smart home | 15–20 years |

### Worm-gear motors
Used primarily for venetian blinds and some heavier exterior systems. Slower but very precise — well suited to applications where slat-angle control matters.

### Battery-powered motors
No mains cabling required — the motor runs from a rechargeable battery that typically lasts 1–2 years per charge depending on usage. Ideal for rental properties or situations where running new cables is impractical.

## Motor force: getting the sizing right

Every motor has a torque rating, expressed in Newton-metres (Nm). Choosing the wrong size is one of the most common installation mistakes.

**General guidance:**

| Blind type | Minimum motor torque |
|---|---|
| Small roller blind (up to 1.5 m wide) | 6 Nm |
| Standard roller blind (1.5–2.5 m wide) | 10 Nm |
| Large roller blind or exterior blind | 20–40 Nm |
| Heavy exterior shutter | 40+ Nm |

Always size the motor for at least 1.5× the calculated load. An undersized motor overheats, wears out prematurely, and voids the warranty.

## Radio protocols: the key decision

If you want smart home integration, the radio protocol is the most critical choice — and the hardest to change later.

### Unidirectional radio
The controller sends commands; the motor has no way to confirm its position. Works fine with a standalone remote but offers limited integration with home automation platforms.

### Bidirectional radio
The motor sends its current position back to the controller. This enables:
- Accurate position feedback in apps and voice assistants
- Automation based on actual blind state (not assumed state)
- Better integration with Google Home, Apple HomeKit, and Amazon Alexa

**Recommendation:** if you have any intention of adding smart home control, choose bidirectional from the start. Retrofitting a different protocol means replacing all the motors.

## Smart home integration at a glance

| Protocol type | Works with |
|---|---|
| Bidirectional + hub | Google Home, Alexa, Apple HomeKit, Home Assistant |
| Native Wi-Fi | Dedicated app, Google Home (via cloud) |
| Unidirectional | Remote control only |

## Noise: what to expect

Motor noise is measured in decibels (dB). The difference between a budget motor and a quality one is significant in practice:

- **Entry-level motors:** 45–55 dB — clearly audible in a quiet room
- **Mid-range motors:** 35–45 dB — noticeable but not intrusive
- **High-end motors:** below 35 dB — barely perceptible

In bedrooms and home offices, noise matters. Do not rely on the manufacturer's stated dB figures alone — ask for references or visit a showroom where you can hear the motor running.

## What to avoid

- **Unknown brands with no European stock of spare parts.** When the motor fails after three years, you will not be able to source a replacement part and will end up replacing the entire unit.
- **Undersized motors.** A motor running at or near its maximum torque constantly will overheat and wear out far sooner than rated.
- **Unidirectional motors in a smart home context.** They work, but poorly — automation becomes unreliable and troubleshooting is difficult.
- **DIY cabling.** Electric blind motors must be connected by a qualified electrician. Non-compliant wiring is a fire hazard and will void building insurance.

## What to ask your installer before committing

1. What is the exact brand, model, and torque rating of the motor?
2. Is the motor bidirectional?
3. Which smart home platforms is it certified to work with?
4. What is the warranty period, and who handles warranty claims?
5. Are spare parts available in Portugal?
6. Is new cabling included in the quote?

## Conclusion

The motor is not the place to economise. A mid-range or high-end motor from a reputable brand, correctly sized for the blind, will give you ten to twenty years of quiet, reliable service. An entry-level motor from an unknown brand may cost half the price but will likely need replacing within five years — at which point you will also pay for labour again.

Invest once, invest well.
